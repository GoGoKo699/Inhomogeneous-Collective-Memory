# Thirty milestones for Inhomogeneous Collective Memory

This map follows the scientific dependencies of the project. It contains exactly **30 milestones**: **12 in background/preliminaries** and **18 in narrative/innovation**. The targets are common to every reader and do not depend on prior knowledge or personal progress. They describe the project's conceptual structure, rather than every calculation or neighboring topic.

The allocation reflects two prerequisite strands: noisy quantum channels with entangled references, and encoding with quantum error correction. The first phase joins those strands into a precise memory task. The second phase moves from a small group of nearby coupling values to the full storage theorem, then to its implementation, extensions, and physical limits. The larger second phase is needed because a small block's fidelity estimate becomes a useful memory only after the correlations, outer code, asymptotic quantifiers, and retained resources have been accounted for.

## One educational anchor and the source boundary

The sole educational anchor is **Michael A. Nielsen and Isaac L. Chuang, *Quantum Computation and Quantum Information*, 10th Anniversary Edition**. “N&C” below refers to that book. The [anchor and source-to-project bridge](anchor_bridge.md) identifies the edition, section map, notation, and missing steps. The [publisher's table of contents](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811070/02173/frontmatter/9781107002173_frontmatter.pdf) verifies the section numbers used here.

Textbook sections provide foundations; the linked scientific pages supply the project's exact statements and proofs. In particular, this map does **not** attribute the specialized singlet/noiseless-subsystem argument, the diamond-norm formulation, the correlated-error memory theorem, or modern Tanner decoding to N&C. Primary research citations are attribution and proof dependencies, not additional educational anchors.

A notation distinction matters throughout: the [bridge defines root state fidelity explicitly](anchor_bridge.md#fidelity-and-complete-channel-error). The project's Bell survival probability and entanglement fidelity are overlaps, equal to the **square** of that root fidelity when the target is a pure Bell state. M07 makes this distinction an explicit understanding target. The publisher's contents verify the section locations; they do not by themselves verify the book's formula conventions.

## Phase I — Background/preliminaries

### M01 — One spin and Pauli rotations

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** What does a weighted Pauli operator describe for one physical spin?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish a quantum state from an observable and from the rotation generated by that observable. Relate the two Pauli axes and their noncommutation to a spin exposed to differently directed fields, and recognize that a coupling coefficient sets strength rather than register size.

**Dependencies and story role:** No earlier milestone. This supplies the local physical object and operator language used throughout both phases.

**Conceptual connection:** A one-spin rotation becomes one term in a collective generator; its coefficient later determines which spins can share an approximately protected block.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.1.3 and 2.1.9. **Project:** [Model Section 1: register, coefficients, and normalization](../science/model.md#1-register-coefficients-and-time-convention), and [the one-spin Bloch evolution](../science/model.md#4-consequence-of-the-theorem).

### M02 — Composite states and inaccessible references

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** What additional information can be present when several spins, or a register and a reference, are entangled?

**Understanding target:** Explain tensor-product state spaces and distinguish a general unknown state from a chosen collection of local states. Understand why preserving entanglement with a reference is part of preserving quantum information, even when that reference is never operated on.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01. The description expands from a single carrier to the joint state whose correlations a memory must preserve.

**Conceptual connection:** Logical information can occupy a subspace across many spins; recovery must preserve its external correlations as well as its local measurement statistics.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.1.7, 2.2.8, and 2.5. **Project:** [Model Section 3: permitted operations and error](../science/model.md#3-permitted-operations-and-the-error-criterion), and [the encoded Bell state in the storage proof](../science/storage_theorem.md#4-sharper-marginal-entanglement-infidelity-bound).

### M03 — Density operators and partial traces

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** What does a marginal state retain, and what information can it omit?

**Understanding target:** Use a density operator to describe a subsystem without assuming that the whole state is uncorrelated. Distinguish the partial trace of a joint state from a claim that the joint state is a tensor product.

**Dependencies and story role:** M02. This provides the local descriptions needed to reason about a large register while keeping its global correlations visible.

**Conceptual connection:** The proof will calculate one-block marginal channels exactly, while retaining correlations between the errors on different blocks.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.4.3 and 8.3.1. **Project:** [Exact block marginals and unused spins](../science/storage_theorem.md#5-exact-non-signalling-and-unused-spins), and [the spectator example](../science/examples.md#3-why-spectator-correlations-do-not-change-a-marginal).

### M04 — Quantum channels and Kraus descriptions

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** How does a quantum channel describe noise without specifying an observed error event?

**Understanding target:** Connect completely positive, trace-preserving maps with an environment and with an operator-sum representation. Distinguish a channel from one particular Kraus description, and recognize that decoding, discarding a subsystem, and replacing leakage by a state are also channel operations.

**Dependencies and story role:** M02–M03. States and their reductions now become inputs and outputs of physical transformations.

**Conceptual connection:** The storage process, inner decoder, randomization, and final recovery are composed as channels, allowing one operational error statement for the whole protocol.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Section 8.2, especially Sections 8.2.2–8.2.4. **Project:** [The common-field representation](../science/model.md#2-what-the-common-field-assumption-means), and [the inner decoder](../science/storage_theorem.md#3-exact-local-diamond-norm-estimate).

### M05 — Common fields versus independent noise

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** Why can identical-looking single-spin disturbances lead to different many-spin memory behavior?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish spins driven by shared random fields from spins driven by independent fields. Explain why knowing every single-spin marginal channel does not determine the joint noise channel, and why a common-field channel need not be a product of its marginals.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01–M04. This identifies the physical correlation assumption before any protection mechanism is introduced.

**Conceptual connection:** Shared noise makes near-equal couplings useful, while an independent local-noise component can impose a qualitatively different lifetime limit.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 8.2.2, 8.3.3–8.3.4, and 10.3.2. **Project:** [Common-field model](../science/model.md#2-what-the-common-field-assumption-means), and [the independent-noise boundary](../science/interpretation.md#the-independent-noise-boundary).

### M06 — Hermitian Lindblad dynamics

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** How does a fixed noisy generator determine the channel at every storage time?

**Understanding target:** Relate a Markov generator to its semigroup of channels and interpret the two weighted Hermitian jumps. Keep the fixed positive rates, unnormalized collective operators, and absence of an additional Hamiltonian explicit. Identify self-adjoint, negative Hilbert–Schmidt dynamics as a special property proved for this model.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01, M04, and M05. The physical noise assumption becomes a mathematical evolution law.

**Conceptual connection:** Hermitian jumps support both the finite-time spectral survival estimate and the exact partial-trace identity used later.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Section 8.4.1. **Project:** [Model Section 1](../science/model.md#1-register-coefficients-and-time-convention), [spectral survival](../science/storage_theorem.md#4-sharper-marginal-entanglement-infidelity-bound), and [Hilbert–Schmidt relaxation](../science/symmetry.md#3-fixed-operators-and-convergence). The special spectral properties are supplied by these project derivations.

### M07 — Fidelity conventions and channel performance

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** Which notion of fidelity is being bounded, and what does that bound measure?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish trace distance, root state fidelity as defined in the bridge, Bell survival probability, and entanglement fidelity of a decoded channel. For a pure Bell target, recognize that this project's overlap $`F_e`$ or $`F_{\mathrm{surv}}`$ equals the square of the root state fidelity. A marginal entanglement-infidelity estimate is not yet a worst-case global channel estimate.

**Dependencies and story role:** M02–M04 and M06. This supplies the performance quantities before the proof uses them.

**Conceptual connection:** The sharper block estimate controls an average Bell overlap; the later Pauli reduction is what turns it into the probability needed for a reference-uniform memory guarantee.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 9.2.1–9.2.3 and 9.3; see the [notation bridge](anchor_bridge.md). **Project:** [Block survival and decoded fidelity](../science/storage_theorem.md#4-sharper-marginal-entanglement-infidelity-bound), and [the exact four-spin example](../science/examples.md#2-an-exact-four-spin-block).

### M08 — Reference-uniform distance and composition

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** What does it mean for a recovered channel to be close to the identity for every entangled input?

**Understanding target:** Interpret half-diamond distance as a worst-case channel error including an arbitrary reference. Distinguish that requirement from a single-state overlap or average fidelity, and connect channel contraction to an additive budget for imperfect encoding and recovery.

**Dependencies and story role:** M02, M04, and M07. This is a **project extension of the textbook foundations**, establishing the final criterion into which local estimates must fit.

**Conceptual connection:** The operational task and the endpoint error budget need one criterion stable under additional quantum systems and channel composition.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 9.2–9.3 provide the state-distance and channel-performance background; **the diamond-norm definition and argument are supplied locally**, not attributed to those sections. **Project:** [Model Section 3](../science/model.md#3-permitted-operations-and-the-error-criterion), [reference-uniform recovery](../science/storage_theorem.md#6-global-pauli-twirling-and-correlated-error-correction), and [endpoint error composition](../science/resources.md#5-a-composable-endpoint-error-budget).

### M09 — Encoded subspaces, leakage, and recovery

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** How can a smaller logical system be stored inside a larger physical one when noise may leave the code space?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish the logical input, its isometric physical image, the full physical Hilbert space, and the decoded output. Explain why the specified decoder includes all leakage outcomes and replaces leaked states by a fixed logical state instead of postselecting them away.

**Dependencies and story role:** M02, M04, and M08. This establishes the input/output interface for a protected block.

**Conceptual connection:** A useful block need not remain perfectly inside its singlet code. Its leakage becomes part of the logical channel seen by outer error correction.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 8.2 and 10.3. **Project:** [Inner encoding and dimension](../science/storage_theorem.md#2-binning-and-the-singlet-code), and [trace-preserving leakage recovery](../science/storage_theorem.md#3-exact-local-diamond-norm-estimate).

### M10 — Pauli weight and correctable error sets

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** What does a quantum code guarantee when it corrects every error below a specified weight?

**Understanding target:** Relate Pauli errors, stabilizer constraints, error syndromes, and code distance. Interpret Pauli weight as the number of affected logical positions and understand why correcting a specified error set does not require those errors to occur independently.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01, M04, and M09. This introduces the outer-code interface without assuming a particular stochastic noise model.

**Conceptual connection:** The project's common fields generate correlated errors; a code correcting every sufficiently low-weight Pauli remains applicable once that weight is controlled.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 10.3, 10.3.1, and 10.5.1. **Project:** [Pauli weight and recovery](../science/storage_theorem.md#6-global-pauli-twirling-and-correlated-error-correction), [the finite outer-code argument](../science/storage_theorem.md), and [correlated five-qubit recovery](../science/examples.md#5-outer-correction-under-an-explicitly-correlated-law).

### M11 — Exact collective singlets and noiseless protection

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** Why can an exactly common rotation leave quantum information inside a spin-zero sector unchanged?

**Understanding target:** Explain the collective-spin annihilation condition and distinguish a one-dimensional singlet state from a singlet sector with enough multiplicity to encode an unknown state. Recognize exact noiseless protection as a special encoded structure, and distinguish it from approximate protection when couplings differ.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01–M02, M06, and M09–M10. This is a **specialist project extension of textbook foundations**, joining symmetry to the coding interface.

**Conceptual connection:** The inner code uses the exact collective structure locally, even though the whole inhomogeneous register may have no exact noiseless subsystem.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.2.8, 10.3, and 10.3.3 support composite systems and degenerate error correction; the specialized singlet/noiseless-subsystem result is **not** assigned to the textbook. **Project:** [Singlet code construction](../science/storage_theorem.md#2-binning-and-the-singlet-code). **Research attribution:** [Zanardi–Rasetti](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9705044) and [the DFS–QECC comparison](../references/related_work.md#1-closest-dfs-and-concatenation-comparisons).

### M12 — The complete operational memory task

**Phase:** Background/preliminaries.

**Central question:** What must a size-dependent quantum-memory claim specify before its lifetime is meaningful?

**Understanding target:** Assemble the physical noise, supplied coupling list, logical rate, storage duration, endpoint operations, inaccessible reference, and error criterion into one task. Count unused physical spins and the independent classical key, and distinguish a classical record from a protected quantum carrier.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01–M11. This is the first phase's synthesis: individual physical and mathematical objects become the complete question addressed by the theorem.

**Conceptual connection:** The next phase must build a protocol that meets this exact interface; a favorable local fidelity or an uncharged auxiliary system would not by itself answer the task.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 9.3, 10.3, and 10.5.8. **Project:** [The operational model](../science/model.md), [implementation interpretation](../science/interpretation.md#what-the-implementation-guarantees-mean), and [the finite theorem statement](../science/storage_theorem.md#1-finite-size-theorem).

## Phase II — Narrative/innovation

### M13 — Coefficient bins for an arbitrary supplied list

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why does a bounded list of couplings guarantee many small groups of closely matched spins?

**Understanding target:** Explain the binning construction, the discarded remainders, and the width of a complete block without assuming random or quasiuniform coefficients. Identify the increasing density in a fixed coupling interval as the resource; the physical noise rates stay fixed.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01, M03, M11, and M12. The second phase begins with the local grouping decision that creates approximately collective blocks.

**Conceptual connection:** A simple coefficient-space construction converts a completely arbitrary supplied list into the geometric control needed by the singlet code.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 8.2.2 and 10.3 supply noise and encoding context; the grouping estimate is local to this project. **Project:** [Exact binning loss](../science/storage_theorem.md#2-binning-and-the-singlet-code), and [the origin of the scale](../science/interpretation.md#the-scale-comes-from-the-coupling-values).

### M14 — Singlet multiplicity and integer logical dimensions

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** How much unknown information fits into a complete block's singlet sector?

**Understanding target:** Relate the Catalan dimension $`D_m`$ to $`k_m=\lfloor\log_2D_m\rfloor`$ logical qubits. Distinguish the full singlet projector from the selected power-of-two code subspace, understand the factor $`\rho_m=D_m/2^{k_m}<2`$, and connect growing block size to inner rate approaching one.

**Dependencies and story role:** M02, M09, M11, and M13. A group of physical spins now receives a precise logical dimension.

**Conceptual connection:** The same dimension choice controls both the storage rate and the trace comparison used in the sharper noise estimate.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.2.8 and 10.3. **Project:** [Singlet multiplicity and logical dimension](../science/storage_theorem.md#2-binning-and-the-singlet-code), and [the two-dimensional four-spin code](../science/examples.md#2-an-exact-four-spin-block). The Catalan count and power-of-two restriction are derived in the project.

### M15 — The exact local Duhamel estimate

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** How does near-equality of couplings produce a channel bound that is valid at finite time?

**Understanding target:** Explain why the collective part annihilates the code and commutes with its same-axis mismatch, giving exact restricted identities for both the jump and its square. Connect those identities to the restricted dissipator bound and Duhamel integration. Distinguish an all-time bound from a truncated short-time expansion.

**Dependencies and story role:** M06, M08, and M13–M14. This establishes the first rigorous local protection estimate.

**Conceptual connection:** The block's special symmetry removes a dangerous linear mismatch contribution while channel contraction prevents the integration from requiring the evolved state to stay inside the code.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 8.4.1 and 9.3 provide dynamical and performance foundations. **Project:** [Exact local diamond-norm estimate](../science/storage_theorem.md#3-exact-local-diamond-norm-estimate). The restricted diamond estimate and its Duhamel proof are supplied by this page.

### M16 — Singlet covariance and coupling variance

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why does the mean squared noise in the singlet sector depend on the variance of the couplings?

**Understanding target:** Connect permutation and rotation invariance of the full singlet projector to its two-spin covariance. Explain how the equal-coupling component cancels in the trace of the weighted square, leaving within-block coefficient variance, and why restriction to the chosen logical subspace costs the factor $`\rho_m`$.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01, M06, M11, and M14. This refines the local disturbance quantity beyond the worst operator norm.

**Conceptual connection:** The full-sector symmetry supplies a sharper average useful for encoding, even though the protocol stores only a power-of-two portion of that sector.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.2.8, 2.4.3, and 8.4.1 supply composite-state and dynamical foundations. **Project:** [Full-singlet average](../science/storage_theorem.md#4-sharper-marginal-entanglement-infidelity-bound). **Research attribution:** [The singlet-moment comparison and Urizar-Lanz et al. source](../references/related_work.md#5-related-sensing-and-the-single-spin-diagnostic).

### M17 — Finite-time Bell survival and decoded infidelity

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** How does the singlet variance control a block's decoded entanglement fidelity for every time?

**Understanding target:** Connect the encoded Bell state, Hilbert–Schmidt spectral positivity, and Jensen's inequality to $`p_b\le1-e^{-t\mu_b}`$. Explain why the leakage decoder retains the survival contribution. Keep this sharper entanglement-infidelity estimate distinct from M15's arbitrary-input local diamond bound.

**Dependencies and story role:** M06–M07, M09, and M14–M16. The local analysis now produces the exact scalar quantity used by the outer proof.

**Conceptual connection:** A spectral property of the Hermitian generator converts an initial variance into a nonperturbative channel-performance bound.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.5, 8.4.1, and 9.2.2–9.3. **Project:** [Spectral survival and the decoded channel](../science/storage_theorem.md#4-sharper-marginal-entanglement-infidelity-bound), and [four-spin evolution](../science/examples.md#2-an-exact-four-spin-block). The spectral-Jensen argument is supplied locally.

### M18 — Closed block marginals with correlated noise

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why is a single-block calculation valid inside the full register without making the blocks independent?

**Understanding target:** Explain the partial-trace cancellation for a Hermitian jump split across a block and its complement. Distinguish the resulting exact marginal-channel identity from factorization of the global channel, and use it to account for spectator and unused spins.

**Dependencies and story role:** M03–M06, M12, and M17. The proof moves from an isolated protected group to many simultaneously stored groups.

**Conceptual connection:** Exact marginal closure makes the block estimates globally relevant while preserving the common-field correlations that the recovery must handle.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.4.3, 8.2.2, and 8.3.1. **Project:** [Exact non-signalling and unused spins](../science/storage_theorem.md#5-exact-non-signalling-and-unused-spins), and [the spectator calculation](../science/examples.md#3-why-spectator-correlations-do-not-change-a-marginal).

### M19 — A keyed global Pauli twirl

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** What does endpoint randomization simplify, and which correlations does it leave intact?

**Understanding target:** Explain how independently chosen logical Pauli keys transform the full decoded channel into a generally correlated Pauli distribution. Relate each block's identity-Pauli probability to its entanglement fidelity, and count the retained classical key. Distinguish the averaged protocol from a guarantee for every key value.

**Dependencies and story role:** M04, M07, M10, M12, and M17–M18. The block fidelity bounds become probabilities in one global error description.

**Conceptual connection:** Randomization supplies a representation useful for correction without assuming that the physical noise events are independent or that the noise path is known.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 8.2.3 and 10.3.1. **Project:** [Global Pauli twirling](../science/storage_theorem.md#6-global-pauli-twirling-and-correlated-error-correction), and [a correlated twirled example](../science/examples.md#4-pauli-twirling-preserves-correlations). The specific correlated-channel reduction is derived in the project.

### M20 — From expected error weight to reference-uniform recovery

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why is an expected error-weight bound enough when errors can be strongly correlated?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish a bad inner block from an erroneous logical-qubit position, including the factor $`k_m`$. Connect the integer Markov denominator $`h+1`$ to the event of exceeding the outer correction radius. Explain why exact correction on the successful Pauli branches gives a half-diamond bound for every logical input.

**Dependencies and story role:** M08–M10 and M19. This is the central passage from local fidelity information to a global operational guarantee.

**Conceptual connection:** An adversarial error-set guarantee plus a probability bound replaces an independence or concentration assumption.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 9.3 and 10.3–10.3.1. **Project:** [Qubit weight and correlated-error correction](../science/storage_theorem.md#6-global-pauli-twirling-and-correlated-error-correction), and [explicit correlated outer recovery](../science/examples.md#5-outer-correction-under-an-explicitly-correlated-law).

### M21 — Finite stabilizer-code existence

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** How can the required correction radius and the number of stored qubits be guaranteed together?

**Understanding target:** Interpret the finite Pauli-ball count, the chosen stabilizer redundancy, and the symplectic counting argument. Connect distance at least $`2h+1`$ to correction of weight at most $`h`$, while distinguishing existence of an outer code from an efficient search or decoder.

**Dependencies and story role:** M10 and M20. The recovery interface receives a quantitative outer code rather than an assumed black box.

**Conceptual connection:** The finite coding count makes the memory's rate explicit and identifies which part of the strongest asymptotic claim is existential.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 10.3, 10.5.1, and 10.5.5. **Project:** [Storage proof Section 7: finite Gilbert–Varshamov argument](../science/storage_theorem.md). **Research attribution:** [Gottesman's stabilizer formalism](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9705052) and [Knill–Laflamme recovery conditions](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevA.55.900).

### M22 — The complete finite-size theorem

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** What precisely has been established after all local and global ingredients are combined?

**Understanding target:** State the theorem's quantifiers over supplied lists, block choices, integer code parameters, storage time, and reference-entangled inputs. Track the discarded physical spins, logical dimension, and key-averaged error without replacing a finite bound by asymptotic notation.

**Dependencies and story role:** M12–M21. This integrates the constructive proof from coefficient bins through outer recovery.

**Conceptual connection:** The separate local estimates, marginal identity, randomization, and code count now become one protocol with an explicit rate and all-time error bound.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 8.2, 9.3, and 10.3 supply the composed-channel and encoded-recovery framework. **Project:** [Theorem 1](../science/storage_theorem.md#1-finite-size-theorem), its [proof route](../science/README.md#the-proof-in-one-route), and [the operational model](../science/model.md).

### M23 — Fixed-rate quadratic and rate-one subquadratic limits

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why do the fixed-rate and rate-one statements require different parameter choices?

**Understanding target:** Distinguish fixed $`R<1`$ and fixed error over $`t\le c_{R,\epsilon}n^2`$ from rate approaching one and error vanishing for every $`t_n=o(n^2)`$. Explain how block size, discard fraction, and outer correction fraction change between these limits, and why the proof supplies achievability rather than an optimal exponent.

**Dependencies and story role:** M14 and M21–M22. The exact finite theorem becomes its principal size-dependent consequence.

**Conceptual connection:** Increasing the number of carriers improves coupling-space resolution; changing the desired overhead and error then determines how slowly the construction's parameters must grow.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 9.3 and 10.3–10.5 provide channel-performance and code-rate background. **Project:** [Explicit asymptotic consequences](../science/storage_theorem.md#8-explicit-asymptotic-consequences), and [the physical origin of the scale](../science/interpretation.md#the-scale-comes-from-the-coupling-values).

### M24 — Deterministic existence at a prescribed time

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** What is gained and lost when the retained random key is removed?

**Understanding target:** Explain the two separate steps: selecting one key with good entanglement fidelity and restricting to a large logical subspace with uniform error control. Track the loss of at most one logical qubit and the square-root error bound. Keep the result restricted to a prescribed duration, without an efficient expurgation or one-code-for-all-times claim.

**Dependencies and story role:** M07–M08, M19, and M22–M23. This clarifies the role of classical randomization after the main theorem is established.

**Conceptual connection:** Average channel performance can support deterministic existence after a dimension sacrifice, but this transformation changes both the operational guarantee and the implementation question.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.5 and 9.3 supply purification and channel-performance foundations. **Project:** [Deterministic expurgation proof](../science/storage_theorem.md#9-deterministic-existence-for-a-prescribed-time), and [the retained-key distinction](../science/model.md#3-permitted-operations-and-the-error-criterion). The expurgation argument is supplied locally.

### M25 — Full interaction algebra and finite-size relaxation

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** How can the same register lack exact noiseless storage and still satisfy the memory theorem?

**Understanding target:** Follow the role of distinct nonzero squared couplings in the Vandermonde recovery of individual spin operators. Distinguish the direct sum of local Lie algebras from the full associative matrix algebra, and connect a scalar commutant to eventual depolarization. Do not infer a size-independent mixing rate from that algebraic conclusion.

**Dependencies and story role:** M01, M06, M11, and M23. The claimed contrast between exact symmetry and finite-time storage becomes a precise mathematical statement.

**Conceptual connection:** The order and quantitative scale of limits matter: finite-size eventual relaxation does not determine what survives when size and storage time grow together.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.1.9 and 8.4.1 provide commutators and master-equation foundations. **Project:** [The complete symmetry argument](../science/symmetry.md), and [the interpretation of the limits](../science/interpretation.md#exact-symmetry-and-finite-time-storage-are-different-statements). **Research attribution:** [Knill–Laflamme–Viola's interaction-algebra framework](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9908066).

### M26 — Which rate/time guarantees have polynomial endpoints?

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Which parts of the memory guarantee come with an explicit efficient endpoint construction?

**Understanding target:** Keep three claims separate: fixed-rate Tanner families give polynomial endpoints for fixed $`R<1`$, fixed error, and $`t\le c_{R,\epsilon}n^2`$; the general rate-one result for every $`t_n=o(n^2)`$ remains existential; growing-field CSS Reed–Solomon codes give polynomial endpoints with rate one and vanishing error for $`t_n=o(n^2/\log^3n)`$. Explain why fixed family parameters and growing field size have different resource roles.

**Dependencies and story role:** M10, M21, and M23. The global existence theorem is separated from its two proved algorithmic realizations.

**Conceptual connection:** A code's correction radius, available lengths, and decoder—not only its encoding rate—determine whether the entire memory protocol has polynomial endpoints.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 10.4.2 and 10.5.8. **Project:** [Tanner family and all-size packing](../science/resources.md#3-a-fixed-rate-tanner-outer-family), [Reed–Solomon alternative](../science/resources.md#6-an-explicit-growing-rate-alternative), and [coding-source attribution](../references/related_work.md#4-coding-and-circuit-ingredients-used-directly). The modern Tanner and precise growing-rate guarantees are research ingredients and project derivations.

### M27 — Calibration and composable endpoint resources

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Which supplied information and operations must be counted to make the construction operational?

**Understanding target:** Explain certified coefficient uncertainty, deterministic grouping from finite data, and the role of $`O(1/n)`$ accuracy in the fixed-rate guarantee. Distinguish classical bit precision from experimental acquisition cost, temporary endpoint ancillas from a quantum bypass, and polynomial ideal circuits from a fault-tolerant implementation. Relate endpoint inaccuracies to the composed channel-error budget.

**Dependencies and story role:** M08, M12–M13, M22, and M26. The algorithmic claim is connected to its explicit physical and computational interface.

**Conceptual connection:** A theorem about a known channel becomes a finite-data protocol only when calibration, addressing, retained records, and implementation error are charged separately.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 8.2, 9.3, and 10.5.8. **Project:** [Certified grouping](../science/resources.md#2-exact-grouping-from-certified-finite-data), [endpoint accounting](../science/resources.md#4-endpoint-circuit-and-classical-computation-accounting), and [composable boundary errors](../science/resources.md#5-a-composable-endpoint-error-budget).

### M28 — Coefficient geometry beyond a shared scalar profile

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** What part of the storage mechanism survives when local amplitudes and directions have several parameters?

**Understanding target:** Explain the vector singlet covariance and grouping in a fixed-dimensional Lipschitz parameter space. Relate its resolution to the achieved scale $`n^{2/r}`$ and the rate-one regime $`t_n=o(n^{2/r})`$. Preserve the fixed number of Hermitian fields and no-Hamiltonian assumption; distinguish this variance proof from the scalar Duhamel commutation argument.

**Dependencies and story role:** M13, M16–M18, M20, and M23. The scalar result is placed inside the broader geometric mechanism that the same proof supports.

**Conceptual connection:** The finite-time survival argument depends on how tightly local coefficient vectors can be grouped, rather than on a single fixed polarization direction. The parameter dimension gives an achievable bound, not a universal optimal exponent.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.2.8 and 8.4.1 supply composite-system and noise foundations. **Project:** [Vector variance and parameter-space grouping](../science/coupling_geometry.md), and [the nonparallel-vector example](../science/examples.md#nonparallel-local-coupling-vectors). The geometry theorem is supplied by these project pages.

### M29 — The independent-noise obstruction

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** Why does a fixed independent depolarizing component stop the diverging lifetime?

**Understanding target:** Connect isotropic local depolarization, its commutation with product rotations, and the entanglement-breaking tensor-product channel. Explain why endpoint recovery and retained classical keys cannot restore reference entanglement after this channel. Distinguish that global obstruction from merely observing entanglement-breaking single-spin marginals of common-field noise.

**Dependencies and story role:** M02–M05, M08, M12, and M23–M27. A physical boundary identifies which correlation assumption carries the achieved advantage.

**Conceptual connection:** The theorem's increasing lifetime is compatible with a constant-time ceiling under a different noise component; it is not a generic self-correcting-memory result.

**Source pointers:** **Anchor:** N&C Sections 2.5, 8.3.4, and 9.3 provide entanglement, depolarization, and preservation foundations. **Project:** [Independent-depolarization calculation](../science/examples.md#7-a-fixed-independent-depolarization-rate-stops-the-divergence), and [the physical boundary](../science/interpretation.md#the-independent-noise-boundary). **Research attribution:** [Ruskai's unital-qubit criterion](https://arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0302032v3), as identified in the project's reference account.

### M30 — The contribution as a whole

**Phase:** Narrative/innovation.

**Central question:** What does this project establish about quantum memory, and which stronger conclusions remain open?

**Understanding target:** Explain the full chain from bounded coupling geometry through approximate singlet blocks and correlated-error recovery to the reference-uniform storage law. Distinguish inherited singlet/DFS and coding ingredients from the combined arbitrary-profile guarantee. Keep exact algebra, existence, efficiency, finite numerical evidence, calibration assumptions, and optimality questions in their respective roles.

**Dependencies and story role:** M12 and M22–M29, with the complete earlier proof route available. This is the second phase's synthesis, connecting the contribution, consequences, and limits rather than introducing another isolated technique.

**Conceptual connection:** Exact symmetry and operational finite-time storage answer different questions. The project's result becomes meaningful only when the rate/time quantifiers and the physical resources are stated together.
